Memory hacks

Shortcuts for remembering notes, notation and rhythm. Each one is tied to a stage, so you know when it starts paying off.

Swaras · stages 1 to 3

Sa and Pa never move

They have no komal or tivra form, which is why the drone plays them. In do re mi they are do and sol.

Four go soft, one goes sharp

Re, Ga, Dha and Ni can be komal (flat). Only Ma can be tivra (sharp) (Clarke).

Sa Re Ga = do re mi

In the major scale (Bilawal, Shankarabharanam) the seven swaras line up one to one with do re mi fa sol la ti. Learn one, get the other free.

Ni leans home

Ni is half a step under taar Sa, so it sounds restless. A note that wants to tip upward into Sa is almost always Ni.

Anchor songs for distances from Sa

Hum the opening of the tune starting on your Sa. The second note lands on the swara. Swap in Indian songs you love: your own anchors stick best.

Reading notation · stage 4

Bhatkhande sargam

Line under = komal, line over Ma = tivra. The line shows which way the note moves: down or up.

The dot points to where the note lives: dot below is the low octave, dot above is the high octave (NIOS).

Carnatic letters

Lowercase s r g m p d n is the middle octave, capitals are the upper octave, a dot after the note is the lower octave, at least in the karnatik.com books. Check each book's key: conventions vary.

Treble clef (higher voices, melodies)

Lines, bottom up: E G B D F, "Every Good Boy Does Fine".

Spaces: F A C E, spells "face".

The clef's curl wraps the G line, which is why it is called the G clef.

Bass clef (lower voices)

Lines: G B D F A, "Good Boys Do Fine Always".

Spaces: A C E G, "All Cows Eat Grass".

Its two dots sit either side of the F line, which is why it is called the F clef.

Rhythm · stage 4, start noticing now

Rhythm is when notes happen and how long they last. The beat is the steady pulse under it, the thing you tap your foot to. Tempo is how fast the beat goes, counted in beats per minute.

The big shortcut

Say it, clap it, then sing it. Speaking a rhythm in syllables before singing frees your attention for pitch. Indian music has used rhythm syllables for centuries, and the Western Takadimi system borrowed them (Takadimi).

Walk the beat

Count "1 2 3 4" while walking, one step per beat. Your legs keep tempo better than your head.

Slow is fast

Practise at 60 beats per minute first. Speed up only once you can do it three times without a slip.

How to make anything stick

Test, don't reread

Pulling a note from memory builds it far more than looking at it again. That is why every drill asks before it tells.

Little and often

Twelve minutes daily beats an hour on Sunday. The review cards space themselves for you.

Mix it up

Mixed questions feel harder than one note at a time, and that difficulty is what makes recognition last.

Sing "aa" first

People sing more accurately on a syllable than with lyrics (Berkowska & Dalla Bella, 2013), so add words last.
